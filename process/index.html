How a Project Unfolds, Stage by Stage

A FORMA project moves through eight structured stages — first contact, site review, concept design, design development, permits, construction, supervision and handover — each with clear inputs, outputs and a defined next step, so nothing depends on guesswork.

A structure, not a formality

Every project is different, but the underlying structure that gets it built well is consistent. We use the same eight-stage process for a small renovation and a full turnkey villa, scaling the detail and duration to the project rather than skipping steps to move faster.

  1. 01

    First conversation

    Understand what you are trying to build, why, and what stage the project is currently at.

    Your input

    Your brief, budget parameters, timeline expectations and any existing documents or land information.

    Outputs

    A clear sense of fit, scope and next steps, communicated honestly even if FORMA is not the right match.

    Next step

    Site visit and survey review.

  2. 02

    Site review

    Assess the land or existing property directly — slope, orientation, access, existing structures and zoning.

    Your input

    Site access and any existing survey, title or building documentation.

    Outputs

    A site analysis summarising the constraints and opportunities that will shape the design.

    Next step

    Concept design.

  3. 03

    Concept design

    Translate the brief and site analysis into massing, spatial planning and material direction.

    Your input

    Feedback on concept options, reference material and priority adjustments.

    Outputs

    Concept drawings and 3D views communicating the design's core logic.

    Next step

    Design development.

  4. 04

    Design development

    Resolve structural, interior, landscape and technical detail against the approved concept.

    Your input

    Sign-off on material, layout and specification decisions as they are presented.

    Outputs

    A coordinated technical design ready for permitting and construction pricing.

    Next step

    Permits and planning.

  5. 05

    Permits and planning

    Prepare and submit the documentation required by the relevant local authority.

    Your input

    Any ownership or authorisation documents required for submission.

    Outputs

    A submitted application and, once received, an approval to proceed to construction.

    Next step

    Construction mobilisation.

  6. 06

    Construction

    Build the project against the approved technical documentation.

    Your input

    Timely decisions on any site queries or specification changes that arise.

    Outputs

    Regular progress reporting and a structurally and technically compliant build.

    Next step

    Supervision and quality review.

  7. 07

    Supervision

    Verify quality and design compliance at defined construction stages, independent of day-to-day site management.

    Your input

    None required — supervision reports are shared directly with you.

    Outputs

    Staged inspection records and a final snagging list before handover.

    Next step

    Handover.

  8. 08

    Handover and aftercare

    Complete final inspections, resolve any snagging items and hand over a finished, documented project.

    Your input

    Final walkthrough and sign-off.

    Outputs

    Completion documentation, warranties and maintenance guidance for ongoing use.

    Next step

    Ongoing support as needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all projects go through every stage?

The structure is consistent, though the depth of each stage scales to the project — a supervision-only engagement, for example, enters at the construction and supervision stages rather than from first conversation.

How much time should we expect between stages?

Timelines depend heavily on project scale and complexity; a realistic schedule is provided once the brief and site survey are complete, rather than a generic estimate applied to every project.

Can we pause the process between stages?

Yes, though we recommend discussing timing openly, since gaps between stages can affect scheduling and, in some cases, permit validity.

Who makes decisions during design development?

Key decisions are presented for your sign-off at each stage; our role is to prepare clear, well-reasoned options rather than decide unilaterally on your behalf.

What happens if we want to change scope partway through?

Scope changes are assessed for their impact on budget, schedule and any already-approved permits, and discussed transparently before being incorporated.

How are remote clients kept informed through this process?

Structured reporting and scheduled video reviews are built into every stage, so remote clients receive the same clarity as those visiting the site regularly.

Ready to see where your project fits in this process?

Tell us your brief and current stage, and we will map out what comes next.
