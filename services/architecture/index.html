Concept design and technical architecture for Koh Phangan

Architecture & Villa Design in Koh Phangan

FORMA provides professional architecture and villa design in Koh Phangan, combining site analysis, tropical climate planning, architectural concepts and detailed technical documentation. The result is a property that is distinctive, practical and buildable, with every decision tested against slope, orientation, wind, privacy and island construction logistics.

Design that starts with the site, not the mood board

Koh Phangan is not a flat, uniform building plot. Slopes fall steeply toward the sea, sun paths swing hard between monsoon seasons, and neighbouring plots can close off a view in a single build cycle. Our architecture practice begins every commission with the land itself: contour surveys, solar and wind studies, access routes and the practical realities of building on an island where materials arrive by boat and truck rather than by highway. Concept sketches follow only once those constraints are understood, so the forms we propose are buildable, not just beautiful on paper.

Scope of work

Site and context analysis

Topographic reading, solar orientation, prevailing wind, drainage, access and neighbouring structures, translated into a design brief before a single wall is drawn.

Concept and schematic design

Massing studies, spatial planning and material direction, presented as drawings and 3D views so the logic of the design is clear before it is committed to detail.

Design development

Structural coordination, envelope detailing, opening and shading strategy, and refinement of every junction that determines how the building will actually perform in the tropics.

Technical documentation

Construction-ready drawings, specifications and coordination sets used for permitting, tendering and site execution.

Materials and climate response

Tropical architecture on Koh Phangan succeeds or fails on its handling of heat, humidity and monsoon rain. We favour material palettes that age honestly in a marine climate — dense timber, natural stone, board-formed concrete and locally sourced clay products — combined with deep overhangs, cross-ventilation and shaded transitional spaces that reduce reliance on mechanical cooling without sacrificing comfort.

Land feasibility before design

A good villa starts with an honest reading of the plot. Before any concept is drawn we review the survey, the title documents you hold, the access road, the slope, the soil indications and the position of neighbouring buildings. The aim is to learn early whether the plot can carry the brief, how much of it is realistically buildable, and where the expensive parts of the site are.

On Koh Phangan this review often changes the brief. A view that looks open today may be closed by a neighbouring build, and a gentle-looking slope may need retaining walls that consume a large part of the budget. Finding this out in the first weeks costs very little. Finding it out during excavation costs a great deal.

Designing for sloped and coastal plots

Many Koh Phangan plots fall steeply toward the sea or climb into the hills. We treat the slope as the main design material rather than a problem to flatten. Stepping the building with the land usually costs less than large cut-and-fill, keeps drainage paths natural and gives each level its own view and privacy.

Coastal plots add salt exposure, wind and, in places, tidal or storm considerations. These decide where openings face, which metals and fixings are used, how far the structure sits from the shoreline and how the ground floor is protected from water.

  • Stepped or split-level massing that follows the contours
  • Retaining structures integrated into the architecture, not added afterwards
  • Drainage routes planned from the first sketch so water leaves the site safely
  • Material and fixing choices for salt-laden air on coastal sites

Ventilation, orientation, shading and moisture

Comfort in the tropics is mostly decided by geometry. We orient living spaces to catch the prevailing breeze, place sleeping areas away from the strongest afternoon sun, and design cross-ventilation paths through the plan so that rooms can breathe without relying on air conditioning all day.

Deep eaves, screens, roof overhangs and shaded terraces keep direct sun and monsoon rain off walls and glazing. Equally important is moisture: raised floors, ventilated roof voids, correct falls on terraces and carefully detailed junctions stop humidity and driving rain from damaging finishes and structure over the years.

From concept to technical drawings

The design moves through clear stages, and you approve each one before the next begins. Concept design fixes the massing, orientation and spatial idea. Schematic design resolves the plans, sections and material direction. Design development coordinates structure, openings and services. Technical documentation then produces the dimensioned drawings, details and specifications that a permit application and a contractor can work from.

The technical set is what separates a pleasant image from a building that can actually be priced and built. It includes plans, elevations, sections, construction details, door and window schedules and finish schedules, coordinated with the structural and building-services drawings prepared by the engineers.

Working with structural and MEP engineers

Architecture, structure and building services have to be designed together. We coordinate with structural and mechanical, electrical and plumbing consultants from design development onward, so that beams do not cut across the view, pipes do not cross bedrooms in the wrong place and the roof can carry the equipment it is meant to carry. Where a licensed professional must sign a drawing, the appropriate Thai-licensed engineer or architect is involved.

Private villas and hospitality developments

We design single private villas, family compounds and small hospitality projects such as a handful of guest villas. Private commissions are led by how the household lives. Hospitality work adds guest circulation, service access, privacy between units, operating costs and phasing, because a small resort is often built in stages while the first units start earning.

Timeline and how we work with you

Design duration depends on size, site complexity and how quickly decisions are made, so we give a schedule after the first site review rather than a fixed number in advance. Typical rhythm: you receive a concept for discussion, comment, we revise, and each approved stage is recorded before the next starts. You always see the work in progress rather than a finished surprise.

Design-only or design-and-build

You can commission FORMA for design only and take the documents to any contractor, or continue with us for construction, supervision or a full turnkey delivery. Design-only gives you the freedom to tender the build. Design-and-build gives one team accountable for both the drawings and how they are built, which removes most disputes about who is responsible for a detail. We will explain both routes against your plot and budget, and the choice stays yours.

What affects the cost of architectural design

Design fees on Koh Phangan vary with site complexity and project ambition rather than floor area alone. Rather than quote a fixed figure with no context, we walk every prospective client through the variables that genuinely move the number.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you design for existing land we already own on Koh Phangan?

Yes. Most commissions start from a plot our clients already hold. We begin with a site visit and survey review to understand access, slope, orientation and any existing structures before any design work starts.

Can you work from an existing concept or sketch we already have?

Yes, we regularly develop client sketches, reference images or a previous architect's early concept into a fully resolved, buildable design, provided the underlying site logic holds up to technical review.

How long does concept design typically take?

Concept design for a single villa typically runs four to eight weeks from a completed site survey, depending on site complexity and the number of design iterations required.

Do you handle structural engineering as well?

Structural design is coordinated through specialist structural engineers we work with regularly on the island; our architectural team manages that coordination so the design and structure evolve together.

Can architecture be commissioned without moving into construction with FORMA?

Yes. Design-only engagements are common. We provide construction documentation suitable for tender to a contractor of your choice, and can support that process as much or as little as you need.

How do you handle privacy on tightly spaced plots?

Through massing, opening placement, level changes and planting rather than solid perimeter walls alone — the aim is privacy that still allows light, air and views to move through the site.

What building regulations apply on Koh Phangan?

Requirements depend on zoning, plot size and building type, and are confirmed with the relevant local authority for each specific site; our Permits & Planning coordination exists to manage this process carefully rather than assume a single blanket rule.

Do you design for hospitality or multi-unit projects as well as private villas?

Yes. The same site-first design method applies to boutique hospitality and multi-villa developments, coordinated closely with our Project Management and Turnkey services where a single point of accountability is needed.
