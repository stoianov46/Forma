Building permit and planning coordination

Building Permits & Planning Support in Koh Phangan

FORMA prepares the drawings and documents needed for a building permit on Koh Phangan and coordinates the engineers and consultants involved in the application. We explain the requirements for your plot in advance. Approval itself is decided by the authorities, so no outcome or timeline is guaranteed.

A process that rewards preparation, not shortcuts

Permit and planning requirements on Koh Phangan depend on zoning, plot characteristics and the specific building type proposed, and are ultimately determined by the relevant local authority rather than by us. What we control is the quality and completeness of the documentation submitted, and clear, honest communication with clients about realistic timelines and requirements — never a promise of a specific approval outcome, which is not ours to give.

Scope of work

Requirement review

Confirming which approvals apply to the specific site, zoning and building type before design finalisation.

Documentation preparation

Compiling drawings, reports and forms to the standard required for submission.

Submission coordination

Managing the submission process and responding to authority queries as they arise.

Liaison through decision

Ongoing communication with the client on status, without overstating certainty around timing or outcome.

Documentation standard

Submission quality materially affects how smoothly an application proceeds. We prepare technical drawings and supporting reports to a consistent, complete standard, cross-checked against the specific site's zoning classification before submission to reduce avoidable queries and delay.

What building permits and planning support includes

Building permits are the formal approvals an authority issues before certain construction work may begin. Our role is preparation and coordination: working out which approvals the plot and project are likely to need, producing the drawings and supporting documents, coordinating the professionals who must contribute or sign, and following the application through.

What we do not do is decide, speed up or promise an outcome. Requirements depend on the plot, its zoning, the type and size of building and the authority handling the application, and they must be confirmed for each project rather than assumed from another one.

Check the plot before you commit to a design

The best moment for planning review is before the design is fixed, and ideally before the land is bought. Zoning, access, proximity to the shore, watercourses or steep ground, and the status of the land documents can all limit what may be built, where and how high.

A preliminary review does not replace the authority's decision, but it reveals the questions that need answers early. It is better to learn that a desired footprint or building type is doubtful during concept design than after the drawings are complete.

How the process runs, stage by stage

First the requirements are identified and written down for your plot, with open questions marked as open. Then the architectural drawings are completed to a level suitable for submission, and structural and services information is added where the application requires it. A licensed engineer or architect signs where the rules call for a signature.

The package is checked internally for completeness and consistency, submitted, and followed. If the authority asks for clarification or changes, we review the request with the design team, answer it and keep you informed. Any conditions attached to an approval are carried into the construction drawings so that they are actually built.

Documents you will usually be asked for

The exact list is confirmed once the project is scoped, but most applications begin with the same kinds of material. Gathering them early avoids the common delay of a missing document discovered at submission.

  • Proof of land ownership or the owner's authorisation to apply
  • A site survey showing boundaries, levels and existing structures
  • Identification of the applicant and any appointed representative
  • Design drawings and the engineering information the authority requires
  • Any previous approvals or records for existing buildings on the plot

Island and site factors that complicate applications

Koh Phangan plots are often sloped, set back from narrow roads or close to the coast and watercourses. These conditions can affect where a building may sit, how drainage is handled and what structural information is needed. Water supply, wastewater handling and road access also have to be workable for the project, not only permitted on paper.

Older plots can carry unclear boundaries or informal access arrangements. Resolving these before submission is slower than ignoring them but far cheaper than having an application stall on them.

Timeline factors, and why we do not quote a date

Duration depends on how complete the submission is, how the authority is working at the time, whether clarifications are requested and whether the project is simple or unusual. Any promise of a specific approval date would be a guess, so we do not make one.

What an owner can control is readiness: complete documents, quick answers to queries and a design that is not still changing. The permit stage should also be placed in the overall programme as a dependency, so that contractor mobilisation and orders are not committed before approvals are in hand.

Mistakes to avoid, and how permits connect to design and build

The common mistakes are buying land without checking what may be built on it, building ahead of approval at the owner's own risk, and changing the design after approval without checking whether the change needs to be notified. Foreign ownership and company structures can also affect the process, and those questions may need separate legal advice outside our scope.

Permit work sits alongside architecture, so the approved drawings and the construction drawings stay consistent, and it feeds project management and supervision, so conditions are tracked on site. Where requirements are uncertain, we say so plainly and recommend confirming them with the authority or a qualified adviser.

What affects permit and planning timelines

We are cautious about quoting fixed timelines, since approval processes depend on the relevant authority and site-specific factors outside our control. The variables below affect how straightforward a given application is likely to be.

  • Zoning classification and any site-specific restrictions
  • Building type, scale and any variance from typical requirements
  • Completeness and quality of the submitted documentation
  • Authority workload and processing times, which vary and are outside our control
  • Whether the site has any existing compliance history to resolve first

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you guarantee my permit will be approved?

No. We do not hold government authority and cannot guarantee an approval outcome; our role is to prepare complete, accurate documentation and manage the submission process professionally.

How long does the permit process typically take?

Timelines depend on the relevant authority and the specific application, and are not something we can commit to in advance; we share realistic expectations based on current known processing patterns once your specific application is scoped.

Do you handle permits for renovation projects too?

Yes, where renovation works require approval, the same documentation and submission coordination process applies.

What happens if the authority requests changes to the design?

We review the request with the design team, prepare a compliant response and resubmit promptly, keeping the client informed throughout.

Can construction start before the permit is approved?

We advise against starting construction ahead of the required approvals; specific circumstances should be discussed directly, as the risk sits with the property owner.

Do you coordinate permits for land we have not yet purchased?

We can provide a preliminary review of likely zoning and approval requirements to inform a purchase decision, though a full application requires confirmed ownership or authorisation.

Are permit requirements different for foreign-owned property?

Ownership structure can affect the applicable process; we recommend discussing your specific situation directly, as this sits partly outside our scope and may require separate legal advice.

What documents do you need from us to start?

Typically land title documents, any existing survey information and confirmation of the intended building brief; we confirm the exact list once your project is scoped.
