Renovation and remodeling for existing properties

Villa Renovation & Remodeling in Koh Phangan

FORMA renovates and remodels existing villas on Koh Phangan, starting with an assessment of the structure, roof, drainage and services before any design is drawn. Work ranges from a cosmetic refresh to a full structural renovation, keeping what performs well and replacing only what does not.

Every renovation starts with an honest assessment

Older properties on Koh Phangan often have real bones worth keeping — a strong structural frame, a good relationship to the site, mature landscaping — alongside genuine problems in layout, ventilation, waterproofing or finish quality. Our renovation process starts by separating the two: a structural and condition survey identifies what can be retained safely, what needs remediation, and what should be reimagined entirely, before any redesign work begins.

Scope of work

Condition and structural assessment

Independent review of structure, waterproofing, services and finishes to establish a realistic scope and budget baseline.

Redesign

Layout, material and technical upgrades developed against the assessment findings and the client's revised brief.

Technical coordination

Structural remediation, MEP upgrades and waterproofing detailing coordinated with specialist consultants where required.

Phased execution

Works sequenced to suit occupied properties or hospitality operations that cannot fully close during construction.

Materials for renovation work

Renovation material choices must integrate convincingly with what remains, particularly where an extension meets an existing structure. We match or deliberately contrast existing stone, timber and concrete finishes with intent, and prioritise waterproofing and moisture management upgrades — frequently the root cause of tropical building deterioration — before any cosmetic finish is applied.

What villa renovation covers and where it stops

Villa renovation covers work on a house that already exists: repairing structure and waterproofing, changing layouts, replacing services, extending, and updating finishes. The scope can be limited to one room or reach through the whole property.

It does not cover problems that the assessment cannot reasonably find, such as concealed defects behind finished walls, so the plan allows for discoveries once opening-up begins. Land, legal title and neighbouring boundary matters are also outside the work and are worth reviewing before design starts.

How a villa renovation proceeds from survey to handover

Renovation reverses the order of a new build. We investigate first, design second and build last. The survey records cracks, damp, roof condition, drainage and the age of electrical and plumbing systems, and the redesign responds to what is found.

During works we open up in stages, so hidden conditions are inspected and recorded as they are exposed. Decisions on repairs are made with you before the scope changes.

Common problems in older tropical villas

Most defects in older Koh Phangan houses trace back to water. Leaking roofs, blocked gutters, failed terrace waterproofing, rising damp and poor drainage around the foundations rot timber, stain walls and corrode reinforcement.

Heat and humidity add steady wear. Salt air attacks metal fixings and window frames near the coast, termites damage untreated timber, and ventilation designed for a different era can leave rooms damp and musty. Fixing these causes comes before any cosmetic work, otherwise new finishes fail for the same reason.

Deciding between renovation, remodeling and rebuilding

A villa renovation is usually sensible when the structure is sound and the location is good. A refresh suits tired finishes, remodeling suits a layout that no longer works, and a deeper renovation suits a building with failing services or envelope but a worthwhile frame.

Rebuilding becomes the honest recommendation when the structure is compromised or when required changes would cost more than starting afresh. We say so after the assessment rather than before it, since photographs rarely tell the full story.

Island logistics and living through a renovation

Renovation on Koh Phangan combines small, awkward jobs with island supply constraints. Materials still travel by ferry or barge, and access to older plots can be tighter than for a new build, which affects what can be delivered and how waste leaves the site.

If you stay in the villa, we plan dust control, water and power shutdowns, and safe routes through the site. Operating guest villas need a schedule that works around bookings. Sequencing in the wet season favours interior and sheltered work while roofing and exterior finishes wait for drier spells.

How renovation works with other FORMA services

A renovation often draws on several services. Interior design reshapes rooms, landscape design repairs the garden around new works, permit coordination handles approval where the changes require it, and construction delivers the work on site.

Supervision can be added if you prefer independent checking of quality, and project management helps where a renovation spans several buildings or an operating property.

Renovation mistakes to avoid

The usual mistake is skipping the investigation and pricing from a walkthrough. The second is covering damp or cracks with paint, which hides the symptom while the cause continues. A third is adding an extension without checking whether the old foundation or structure can carry it.

Other common errors are mixing new and old materials without a plan for how they meet, ignoring drainage when levels change, and starting finishes before waterproofing and services are tested. A clear scope, a reserve for discoveries and decisions made before demolition prevent most of them. Agree early which existing elements stay, since retaining a wall, floor or roof shapes both the design and the order of work.

What affects renovation cost

Renovation costs are harder to estimate from photographs alone than new-build costs, because so much depends on hidden condition. We are direct about that uncertainty and resolve it through assessment before committing to a number.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you renovate a villa while we continue living in it?

Often yes, through careful phasing, though the extent of works and the layout of the property determine how much disruption is realistic to avoid.

Do you renovate hospitality properties without closing them fully?

Yes, phased renovation for operating villas and small hospitality properties is a regular part of our work, planned around occupancy and booking calendars where possible.

What if the structural survey finds serious problems?

We present the findings clearly, alongside realistic remediation options and cost implications, so the decision to proceed, adjust scope or reconsider stays fully informed and in your hands.

Can a renovation include a significant layout change?

Yes, provided the existing structure can support it or can be reasonably modified; this is assessed during the structural review before redesign begins.

How do you handle water damage and waterproofing failures?

These are treated as priority items — the underlying cause is diagnosed and resolved before new finishes are applied, rather than concealing a recurring problem cosmetically.

Do you handle permits for renovation work?

Where the scope of works requires approval, permit coordination is managed through our Permits & Planning service alongside the renovation project.

Can existing furniture and fittings be reused?

Frequently, yes — we assess what is worth retaining and design the new interior around it where it fits the revised scheme.

How long does a typical villa renovation take?

Timelines vary widely with scope, from a few weeks for a cosmetic refresh to several months for structural and full-layout renovations; a project-specific schedule follows the assessment.
