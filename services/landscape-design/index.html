Tropical landscape and outdoor living design

Landscape Design for Tropical Gardens & Pools in Koh Phangan

FORMA designs tropical gardens, pool surrounds and outdoor living areas on Koh Phangan as an extension of the architecture. The design covers planting suited to the soil and climate, drainage, retaining walls, shade and lighting, so the landscape works through the monsoon and matures well over time.

The garden is not what surrounds the house — it completes it

On a tropical island, the boundary between inside and outside is one of the most important design decisions a project makes. A pool positioned without regard to the living room's sightlines, planting that blocks the very breeze the architecture was designed to capture, or drainage left unresolved until construction — these are the failures we design against from the outset, treating landscape as a discipline coordinated with architecture rather than an afterthought applied once the building is finished.

Scope of work

Pool and outdoor living design

Pool positioning, decking and shaded outdoor rooms designed as continuous space with the interior.

Planting strategy

Species selection for climate resilience, privacy, shade and low ongoing maintenance.

Pathways and hardscape

Circulation, steps and retaining details resolved for the site's actual slope and drainage pattern.

Drainage and water management

Surface water strategy that protects the building and prevents erosion on sloped sites during monsoon rainfall.

Planting and materials for tropical gardens

We favour planting palettes that are genuinely resilient to Koh Phangan's conditions — drought-tolerant once established, tolerant of intense sun and heavy seasonal rain, and selected as much for structure and shade as for flowering display. Hardscape materials are chosen for slip resistance around pools and durability against constant UV and moisture exposure.

What landscape design includes and where it stops

Landscape design covers everything outside the walls that has to be planned: levels, paths, steps, retaining walls, decks, pool surroundings, planting, outdoor lighting, irrigation and the way water leaves the site. It produces a plan and technical details that a landscape contractor can price and build.

Pool structure and mechanical systems are specialist work, so we coordinate with pool builders rather than replace them. Structural retaining walls are checked by an engineer. Our role is to make sure all of these pieces fit together into one coherent outdoor space.

How landscape design moves through stages

The work starts with a site reading: slope, existing trees, soil, sun and wind, and where water currently runs during heavy rain. A concept follows, showing how the garden relates to the house, where people will sit and how they move through the plot.

Once the concept is agreed, we develop planting and hardscape in detail and then document levels, drainage and construction details. If you continue with us, we coordinate during construction so that planting and paving do not clash with building works.

Planning drainage and slopes in monsoon conditions

On a tropical island, landscape design is mostly water management. Rain can fall in short, heavy bursts that strip bare soil from a slope within a single season. We plan swales, drains and soakaways early, and use planting, terracing and stone to slow the flow instead of simply piping it away.

Water must never be directed toward foundations or a neighbour. This is why landscape levels are settled together with the villa floor levels, not after the house is built.

Choosing plants for heat, wind and salt

Plant choice depends on exposure. Coastal plots need species that tolerate salt spray and strong wind, while sheltered hillside plots can support broad-leaved shade planting. We avoid species with invasive roots near pools, drains and walls, and favour plants that handle both the dry months and the wet season without constant replacement.

Mature size matters. A small tree planted too close to a terrace can later block the breeze that the building was designed to catch. We place trees with their final canopy in mind.

Designing pools, decks and outdoor rooms

The position of the pool affects views, privacy, light on the water and how far guests walk from the kitchen. We test these before fixing it. Decking and paving are selected for grip when wet and for temperature underfoot, since dark stone in full sun becomes uncomfortable. Shaded areas, pergolas and planted screens turn the edge of the pool into a usable room.

Landscape design with architecture, construction and timeline

Landscape works best when planned with the villa from the start, and we coordinate it with our villa design and architecture services. For a building already under construction, we adapt the garden to existing levels and drains. Our construction team can build the scheme, or the documents can go to a contractor of your choice.

The design period depends on plot size and complexity. Planting has its own rhythm: it should be installed when rain can help it establish, and heavy construction traffic should finish first to avoid damaging new beds. We advise on sequence and, for new gardens, give guidance for the first growing seasons. We do not promise a delivery date before the site and scope are confirmed.

Common landscape design mistakes to avoid

Gardens fail in predictable ways. The most frequent causes are:

  • Leaving drainage until planting is nearly complete
  • Planting large trees too close to the house or pool
  • Choosing plants from a catalogue without checking salt and wind exposure
  • Using smooth paving around the pool that becomes slippery in rain

Lighting, irrigation and maintenance in the landscape

Outdoor lighting should guide movement and highlight planting without flooding the plot or disturbing neighbours and wildlife. Low fittings along steps and paths, shielded sources and warm tones are our usual approach, with cables and fixtures rated for moisture and salt.

Irrigation is planned to establish new planting and then reduce, using drip lines and zones matched to plant needs. A garden that depends on constant hand watering rarely survives the dry season, so we agree a realistic maintenance routine with you during design and shape the planting around it.

What affects landscape design cost

Landscape budgets vary with site scale, slope complexity and the extent of hardscape versus planting in the design.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can landscape design be added to an existing villa?

Yes, we regularly design landscape schemes for existing properties, working with the current architecture and site conditions.

How do you handle drainage on steeply sloped sites?

Through a combination of graded surfaces, retaining structures and managed drainage channels designed specifically for the site's contours and monsoon rainfall intensity.

What plants actually thrive on Koh Phangan?

A range of tropical species tolerant of intense sun, heavy seasonal rain and, near the coast, some salt exposure; we select species specific to each site's microclimate rather than a fixed generic list.

Do you design the pool itself or only the surrounding landscape?

Pool positioning and form are typically resolved with the architecture; detailed pool engineering is coordinated with specialist pool contractors alongside our landscape design.

How much maintenance will the garden need?

We design deliberately for realistic maintenance capacity, discussed with the client upfront, favouring resilient planting over high-maintenance formal schemes unless specifically requested.

Can landscape design help with privacy from neighbouring plots?

Yes, strategic planting is one of the most effective and attractive ways to manage privacy without resorting to solid walls that block breeze and views.

Do you handle irrigation system design?

Yes, irrigation is planned as part of the landscape technical documentation, sized appropriately for the planting palette and site conditions.

How long until a new garden looks established?

Tropical planting grows quickly with the right species selection; a garden typically reads as established within one to two growing seasons, depending on the specimen sizes planted initially.
