Interior design for villas and hospitality spaces

Interior Design for Villas & Hospitality in Koh Phangan

FORMA provides interior design for villas and small hospitality projects on Koh Phangan, covering space planning, material and finish selection, joinery, lighting and furniture. Every specification is checked against heat, humidity and salt air, so interiors keep their quality long after handover without constant maintenance.

Interiors planned with the building, not after it

An interior that feels resolved rarely happened by accident at the end of a project — it was considered from the moment ceiling heights, window placement and structural grids were first drawn. We develop interior concepts in parallel with architecture wherever possible, so joinery, lighting and material transitions align with the building's logic instead of fighting it. Where a client already has an existing shell, we work backward from that structure to find the most honest interior expression it can support.

Scope of work

Concept and material palette

A coherent material and colour direction developed from the site's light, climate and the architecture's character.

Lighting design

Layered lighting strategy for ambient, task and accent needs, tuned for both daylight-heavy tropical living and evening atmosphere.

Joinery, kitchens and bathrooms

Custom cabinetry, kitchen and bathroom detailing specified for durability in a humid marine climate.

Furniture and styling direction

Furniture layout, sourcing guidance and styling that supports daily use as much as photography.

Material and lighting approach

Interior materials on Koh Phangan need to resist humidity and salt air while still feeling warm underfoot and to the touch. We favour natural stone, dense hardwood, woven natural fibres and honest metal finishes that patina rather than corrode. Lighting is layered — recessed and cove lighting for ambient calm, directional fittings for task areas, and warm accent lighting that carries the interior gracefully into evening use around pools and terraces.

What interior design covers for a villa or small hotel

Interior design here means the decisions that make a finished building usable and coherent: room layouts and furniture positions, the palette of surfaces, built-in joinery, kitchens and bathrooms, lighting, and the selection of furniture and fittings. It also includes the drawings and schedules that let a carpenter or supplier build exactly what was intended.

It does not replace architecture or structural work. If walls need to move, the change is coordinated with the architect and engineer, and the interior package adapts to the building rather than assuming it can be altered at will.

How an interior design project moves from idea to installation

We start by learning how each room will be used, then build a concept that shows mood, materials and layout together. After approval the concept is turned into construction information: joinery drawings, lighting positions, switch and socket locations, and a finishes schedule that names every surface.

Procurement and installation come last. Furniture and fittings are ordered against the schedule, delivered to the island, checked on arrival and placed on site so that small adjustments can be made while the design team is still present.

Choosing materials and lighting for humid, salt-laden interiors

Interior design in Koh Phangan has to assume moisture. Plywood with the wrong glue swells, cheap hinges rust within a season, and fabric left in a closed wardrobe can grow mould. We specify marine-grade or properly sealed substrates for wet areas, stainless or coated hardware near the coast, and finishes that can be wiped, refinished or replaced without demolishing the cabinet.

Air movement matters as much as material choice. Ventilated wardrobe backs, gaps behind built-in units and extraction in kitchens and bathrooms keep surfaces dry, which extends the life of everything installed.

Tropical interiors have strong daylight and dark evenings, so lighting must cover both. We plan layers: soft general light, focused light where people read or cook, and low accents for evenings on the terrace. Fixtures near open walls and pools need suitable protection against insects, moisture and salt, and warm light levels are kept low enough to avoid attracting swarms to the living area.

Working with FORMA architecture and landscape teams

When interior design runs alongside our villa design or architecture work, ceiling heights, window sills, niches and service routes are settled before the structure is drawn. That is far cheaper than cutting channels into finished concrete. Where the garden meets the living room, we coordinate with landscape design so that floor levels, thresholds and lighting continue outside.

For existing buildings, we start from a survey of what is there and design around constraints such as low ceilings or fixed plumbing instead of ignoring them.

Interiors for rental and hospitality use

Guest accommodation takes more wear than a private home. Corners, bed heads, luggage zones and bathroom floors need robust finishes, and layouts should make daily cleaning quick. We discuss housekeeping routines and storage needs during the brief so that the scheme looks good and survives repeated turnover.

What affects the interior design timeline

The largest factors are the amount of custom joinery, the origin of furniture and fittings, and how early the design starts relative to construction. Imported items travel further and take longer to reach the island, so long-lead pieces are identified and ordered early. We confirm a schedule once the scope is clear and do not commit to dates that depend on third-party shipping.

Common interior design mistakes to avoid

Most problems come from treating interiors as decoration added at the end. Typical mistakes include:

  • Choosing finishes from photographs without testing them in local humidity
  • Ordering furniture before room dimensions are confirmed on site
  • Placing sockets and lighting after walls are closed
  • Using untreated timber or low-grade hardware in bathrooms and kitchens

Kitchens and bathrooms: where interior design is tested hardest

These rooms combine water, heat, grease and daily use, so layout and detailing matter more than surface appearance. We position sinks, appliances and storage for a natural working sequence, specify falls and waterproofing coordinated with the builder, and choose worktops and tiles that tolerate staining and cleaning products.

Concealed plumbing access, removable panels and cabinets raised off the floor make later repairs simple. Open kitchens common in tropical villas also need strong extraction, because cooking smells and moisture otherwise travel straight into the living area.

What affects interior design and fit-out cost

Interior budgets swing widely with specification level and scope. We explain the real drivers so decisions can be made deliberately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you design interiors for villas you did not architect?

Yes, we regularly develop interiors for existing shells and villas designed by other architects, working from the building's existing logic and structural constraints.

Can interior design run in parallel with construction?

Ideally, yes. Coordinating interior joinery, services and finishes during construction avoids costly rework and produces a more integrated result than adding interiors after handover.

Do you source furniture internationally or only locally?

Both, depending on budget and timeline; Thailand and the broader region offer strong furniture and joinery craftsmanship that we use extensively, supplemented by imported pieces where a specific look requires it.

How do you handle humidity and material durability?

Material selection accounts for the marine tropical climate from the outset — moisture-resistant substrates, appropriate sealants and finishes, and ventilation strategy for cabinetry and wet areas.

Can you design interiors for a hospitality villa with guest turnover?

Yes, with attention to durability, ease of maintenance and cleaning, and finishes that hold up under higher use than a private residence.

Do you provide full styling for photography and launch?

Yes, final styling is part of our handover process for clients who want the space presentation-ready from day one.

What is the typical timeline for interior design?

Concept through to finished specification typically runs several weeks to a few months depending on scope and the number of custom joinery items involved.

Can we retain some existing furniture in a new interior scheme?

Absolutely — we frequently design around pieces clients want to keep, integrating them into a coherent new palette rather than requiring a full replacement.
